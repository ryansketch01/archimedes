# Google GTIG: First Known In-the-Wild AI-Generated Zero-Day Exploit (2026-05-11)

## Summary

Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) on 2026-05-11 disclosed what it
calls the first known in-the-wild use of AI for zero-day exploit
generation in a planned mass-exploitation campaign. The exploit targeted
an unnamed open-source web administration tool to bypass two-factor
authentication; Google states it intercepted the attack before broad
weaponization and is in coordinated disclosure with the affected vendor.

## Analytic notes (from analyst review)

ACH on the central claim ranks H2 (AI-assisted refinement of a human-discovered
or known-class vuln) and H3 (AI-generated PoC for a known-class bug) ahead of
H1 (GTIG's "novel AI-discovered zero-day" framing). All three are consistent
with the structural fingerprints GTIG cites (hallucinated CVSS, code shape,
Gemini exclusion); the diagnostic question — discovery vs refinement vs
PoC-generation — is not resolved by the publicly available evidence. The
2FA-bypass-on-web-admin-tool vulnerability class is well-trodden enough that
the strongest framing (H1) loses parsimony to H2/H3. H1 cannot be ruled out;
GTIG may hold non-public evidence that distinguishes it.

KAC surfaces eight assumptions, one classified as Test (whether the underlying
vuln class is genuinely novel — resolves on CVE assignment), four as Qualify
(GTIG visibility into actor tooling, methodology false-positive rate,
"first in-the-wild" depends on cross-vendor visibility, narrative-shaping
risk from Google's co-published defender piece), three as Sound. No assumption
blocks publication, but the qualifying caveats should appear in the brief.

Briefer should attribute the AI-generation claim to GTIG ("Google states",
"GTIG assesses") rather than presenting it as settled. WEP ceiling stays
at "likely" per grader; the assessment is highly brittle to single-source
dependence and the grader's veto is correctly applied.
