# Bitdefender attributes Azerbaijani oil & gas multi-wave Exchange intrusion (Dec 2025 — Feb 2026) to FamousSparrow (Salt Typhoon alias)

## Summary

Bitdefender Labs disclosed today 2026-05-13 a three-wave intrusion against an unnamed Azerbaijani oil and gas company from late December 2025 through late February 2026, attributing the campaign with moderate-to-high confidence to **FamousSparrow** — a listed alias for **Salt Typhoon** (roster id 010, HIGH threat level, China MSS) per `_roster.yaml`. The attackers re-exploited the same Microsoft Exchange entry point across three waves, rotating between Deed RAT (Snappybee), TernDoor (delivered by Mofu Loader), and a modified Deed RAT variant with updated magic value 0xFF66ABCD and Deflate compression. Bitdefender documents seven distinct tradecraft observations new to the public corpus for this actor, including evolved DLL sideloading via legitimate LogMeIn Hamachi binaries with exported-function override. Hacker News and Dark Reading relay the disclosure; both cite Bitdefender and add no independent evidence basis, so single-source veto applies and WEP caps at "likely". Archimedes Splunk first-party telemetry is clean across both indexes over 30d on the full IOC set.

## Technical detail

**Intrusion timeline (three waves, single Exchange entry point):**

- **Wave 1 (December 25, 2025):** Initial compromise via Exchange exploitation. Deed RAT (Snappybee) deployment. C2: `virusblocker[.]it[.]com:443`.
- **Wave 2 (late January / early February 2026):** Mofu Loader (shellcode loader attributed to GroundPeony) delivers TernDoor. TernDoor includes kernel driver component `vmflt.sys` — first observed 2024 against South American telecommunications; this is the **target-set expansion** to South Caucasus energy.
- **Wave 3 (late February 2026):** Modified Deed RAT variant with magic value `0xFF66ABCD` and Deflate compression replacing prior Snappy compression. C2: `sentinelonepro[.]com:443` — brand-impersonation domain registered 2026-02-26 (same day as Wave 3 onset; strong campaign-specific infrastructure signal).

**Initial access vector:** ProxyShell + ProxyNotShell exploit chain against unpatched Exchange Server.
- ProxyShell: CVE-2021-34473 (CVSS 9.8), CVE-2021-34523 (CVSS 9.8), CVE-2021-31207 (CVSS 7.2)
- ProxyNotShell: CVE-2022-41040 (CVSS 8.8), CVE-2022-41082 (CVSS 8.8)

All five CVEs verified in NVD; all long-patched (2021/2022). This is **n-day recycling** by a tracked APT against an unpatched victim, not a fresh zero-day. The persistence pattern (three waves across two months through the same entry point) is a **victim-hygiene story as much as a tradecraft story** — repeated remediation without full eviction allowed return access.

**Post-exploitation TTPs:**
- `w3wp.exe` (IIS worker process) under `MSExchangePowerShellAppPool` writing web shells to publicly accessible Exchange directories.
- Web shell filenames: `key.aspx`, `log.aspx`, `errorFE_.aspx`, `signout_.aspx`.
- Evolved DLL sideloading via legitimate LogMeIn Hamachi binaries (`LMIGuardianSvc.exe` + `LMIGuardianDll.dll` + `.hamachi.lng`) — overrides two specific exported functions to gate payload execution through the host application's natural control flow for sandbox evasion. Bitdefender characterizes this as a meaningful evolution from prior public FamousSparrow / Salt Typhoon DLL sideloading patterns.

**MITRE ATT&CK mapping (per Bitdefender):** T1190 (Exploit Public-Facing Application), T1505.003 (Web Shells), T1543.003 (Windows Service), T1574.002 (DLL Side-Loading), T1140 (Deobfuscate/Decode), T1562 (Impair Defenses), T1569.002 (Service Execution), T1059.001 (PowerShell), T1021.001 (RDP), T1021.002 (SMB / Admin Shares — Impacket), T1071.001 (HTTPS C2), T1014 (Rootkit — `vmflt.sys`).

**Attribution language preserved (Bitdefender, paraphrased per Hard Rule 6):** Bitdefender's published attribution states the intrusion is best attributed to FamousSparrow with moderate-to-high confidence, based on combined weight of observed TTPs, malware families, and execution flow.
