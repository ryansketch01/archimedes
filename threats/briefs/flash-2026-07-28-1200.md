# ⚡ FLASH: Kaspersky ties three new espionage tools to tracked Iranian actor UNC1549 — victims abroad, but the tradecraft ports to A&D estates

*2026-07-28 12:00 EDT · A2 · WEP: likely · TLP:CLEAR · Posted (inside active hours)*

**What.** Kaspersky Securelist attributes a fresh multi-region espionage campaign to Iranian state-backed **UNC1549** (Nimbus Manticore; roster #004), introducing three previously undocumented tools: **NightLedger**, a Windows backdoor loaded via DLL search-order hijacking against a legitimate `AppVShNotify.exe` and masquerading as `SspiCli.dll`; **BridgeHead**, a SOCKS5 tunnel proxy (`unbcl.dll`); and **ArcBridge**, a WebSocket tunneler that turns victim hosts into covert relays. The attribution restates Kaspersky's established tracking — Archimedes does not originate it.

**Impact.** No US aerospace, defense, or watchlist entity is named. Kaspersky's victims are aviation and telecom organizations across Egypt, Jordan, Tanzania, Pakistan, Ethiopia, and Burkina Faso — A&D relevance is sector-structural, not observed targeting of the target profile. The watch-item is tradecraft portability: DLL search-order hijacking (MITRE T1574.001) against a legitimate Microsoft App-V binary, plus SOCKS5/WebSocket covert tunneling, port cleanly to an ITAR Windows/M365/edge estate.

**Confidence — read the caveat.** A2 / likely. Kaspersky is the sole effective source — The Hacker News and TechNadu relay the same research, and the Broadcom/Symantec bulletin covers prior UNC1549 tooling (MiniFast), not this set. The single-source veto caps confidence at "likely"; the Kaspersky primary was not directly retrieved this sweep.

**Action.** Hunt the search-order-hijack pairing — `SspiCli.dll` loaded from a non-System32 path alongside `AppVShNotify.exe` — not the legitimate DLL names alone, which carry high false-positive risk. Flag anomalous `unbcl.dll` and outbound WebSocket tunneling from workstations. Network IOCs (C2, hashes) sit in the Kaspersky primary, pending direct retrieval.
