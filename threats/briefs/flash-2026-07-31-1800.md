# ⚡ FLASH: MSTIC ties captive-portal Wi-Fi credential theft against corporate travelers to a new Midnight Blizzard sub-cluster

*2026-07-31 18:00 EDT · Digraph: A2 · WEP: likely · TLP:CLEAR*

**What:** Microsoft Threat Intelligence (MSTIC) disclosed CaptiveCrunch — active since early May 2026 — in which a newly designated cluster it tracks as Storm-2945 compromises hospitality and conference Wi-Fi worldwide, funneling corporate travelers through actor-controlled infrastructure for adversary-in-the-middle (AitM) device-code phishing and fake-update malware delivery. Per MSTIC, Storm-2945 is an assessed sub-cluster of Midnight Blizzard (APT29 / Cozy Bear, SVR, roster #009).

**Impact:** No A&D or watchlist victim is named — targeting is corporate travelers broadly — but an ITAR/DIB executive on a compromised hotel or conference Wi-Fi sits inside the victim envelope. The AitM strand abuses the Microsoft Entra ID device-code flow to register attacker devices and collect M365 data, turning one poisoned captive portal into standing tenant access.

**Attribution discipline:** The Storm-2945 → Midnight Blizzard binding rests on MSTIC alone (assessed on overlap with its Storm-2372 device-code cluster); single-source veto caps this at *likely*. ReliaQuest (2026-07-23) corroborates only the doppelganger-domain / device-code layer, not the actor attribution. Archimedes reports MSTIC's assessment, not established fact.

**Action (next hour):**
- **Travelers:** treat venue captive portals as hostile — connect the corporate VPN before authenticating, and reject every "browser/OS update" or ClickFix prompt on hotel/conference Wi-Fi.
- **Identity team:** apply Conditional Access to block or tightly scope the Entra device-code flow; alert on unexpected Entra device registrations and new-device M365 sign-ins.
- **SOC:** hunt the finding's 12 net-new IOCs (4 doppelganger M365 domains, 6 C2 IPs, 2 SHA-256). First-party Splunk: 0 tracked-IOC hits across both indices over -24h (visibility-bounded, not disconfirming).

**Sources:** [MSTIC — CaptiveCrunch disclosure](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/31/captivecrunch-midnight-blizzard-targets-travelers-worldwide-for-malware-delivery-and-credential-theft/) (A) · ReliaQuest, 2026-07-23 (B, partial — device-code / doppelganger-domain layer only).
