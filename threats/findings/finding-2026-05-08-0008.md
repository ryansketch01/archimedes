# RansomHouse publicly claims Trellix breach via darkweb leak-site posting; Trellix acknowledges 'claims of responsibility'

## Summary

BleepingComputer reported on 2026-05-08T13:23 UTC that the RansomHouse data-extortion group has publicly claimed responsibility for the Trellix breach disclosed on 2026-05-01 (covered in finding-2026-05-04-0004 at B3, and surfaced again this morning via SecurityWeek as raw-2026-05-08-am-007 / rejected reject-2026-05-08-0002). RansomHouse posted screenshots of what it describes as the Trellix appliance management system on its darkweb extortion portal, asserts an intrusion date of 2026-04-17, and claims the breach "resulted in data encryption." Trellix has issued a follow-up statement acknowledging it is "aware of claims of responsibility for the attack and are looking into it" but has neither confirmed RansomHouse's identity nor confirmed the encryption claim; Trellix's earlier denial of source-code release / distribution-process compromise stands.

This finding promotes the procedural fact "RansomHouse publicly claimed responsibility for the Trellix breach" at B2 / likely. It does NOT promote the substantive claim that RansomHouse actually compromised Trellix — that requires Trellix confirmation or independent A/B-grade vendor research linking RansomHouse to the screenshots. Per Hard Rule 2, all attribution language must be "per BleepingComputer / per RansomHouse self-claim" — never bare "RansomHouse breached Trellix."

The morning AM-007 raw-signal was rejected due to lack of public attribution, no A&D nexus, and single-source. The afternoon PM-005 piece adds the public attribution, intrusion date, encryption claim, and Trellix follow-up — material new content that warrants reconsidering the cluster as a procedural-fact-only finding without crossing Hard Rule 2.

## A&D-target relevance

**Direct relevance: low.** Trellix is a security vendor (post-merger McAfee Enterprise + FireEye), not an A&D prime.

**Indirect relevance: meaningful.** Trellix products (NDR/EDR + endpoint legacy) are widely deployed in federal, DoD, and A&D environments as endpoint defense and threat intelligence. Source-code compromise of a security vendor creates two potential supply-chain risks:
1. Update / patch poisoning if attackers can move from source repo to build/release pipeline (Trellix denies evidence of this)
2. Detection-bypass discovery — with source code, an attacker can identify which behaviors Trellix detects and engineer evasion

The April 17 intrusion date plus May 8 public claim = ~3 weeks intrusion-to-claim, standard data-extortion operating tempo.

**Authenticity caveat:** BleepingComputer notes "could not confirm the authenticity of the data" in RansomHouse's screenshots. Trellix has not confirmed the encryption claim. WEP "likely" applies to the procedural fact of the public claim — NOT to breach scope, encryption truth, or supply-chain compromise risk.
