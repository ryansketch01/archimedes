# Operation Silent Rotor — Rust-based malware targets Eurasian unmanned aviation sector ahead of Moscow drone forum; Boeing-aliased lures

## Summary

Seqrite Labs published primary research on 2026-05-08 naming "Operation Silent Rotor" — a spear-phishing campaign delivering a Rust-based 64-bit Windows executable disguised as order confirmation documents from the Russian Aeronautical Information Center (Центр аэронавигационной информации / ЦАИ / CAI). Targets are professionals in the Eurasian unmanned aviation systems sector. The campaign is timed to the XIII Eurasian International Forum "Unmanned Aviation 2026" held in April 2026 in Moscow. Lure documents in the malicious archive include Boeing 737 navigation databases and NOTAM (Notice to Airmen) datasets — Tier-1 watchlist alias surface, though Seqrite frames these as social-engineering content rather than confirmed Boeing intrusion. SecurityWeek's "In Other News" column carries a one-line pointer to Seqrite's primary research.

Seqrite explicitly does not attribute the campaign to any known threat actor. Russian-language lures and regional targeting suggest a Russian-language operator but origin / sponsor is unspecified. Per Hard Rule 2, Archimedes records Seqrite's non-attribution verbatim and does not originate attribution.

## Targeting (per Seqrite)

- **Industries:** Unmanned Aviation Systems (UAS/UAV), aeronautical information services
- **Geographies:** Russia, Tajikistan, Central Asia, Middle East, Europe
- **Lure documents (decoys in the malicious archive):**
  - Translation certificate from a Tajikistan-based company
  - Aviation product order confirmation
  - Excel spreadsheets listing Boeing 737 navigation databases and NOTAM datasets

The Boeing 737 navigation database lure is subject-matter content within the malicious archive — Seqrite frames the malware's social engineering as centered on Boeing aircraft navigation data, NOT a confirmed Boeing intrusion. Archimedes records the Boeing-aliased watchlist surface for awareness; the finding does not claim Boeing was targeted as an organization.

## Malware (per Seqrite)

**Stage 1 — Initial execution:**
- 64-bit Rust executable
- System fingerprinting: hostname + C: drive volume serial number XOR'd
- Collects environment variables (username, domain, profile path) + network adapter info
- JSON exfiltration via XOR encryption over HTTPS to C2

**Stage 2 — Payload delivery:**
- Receives encrypted response, decrypts via AES-256 in blocks
- Writes randomly-named .exe to `%USERPROFILE%\Documents\` or `C:\Users\Public\Documents\`
- Executes second-stage payload

## Infrastructure (per Seqrite)

- **C2 domain:** cdn[.]kleymarket[.]ru (registered ~9 days before Seqrite analysis)
- **Primary C2 IP:** 45[.]142[.]36[.]76 (AS48347 MTW-AS, Moscow)
- **Historical IPs:** 92[.]62[.]113[.]232, 89[.]108[.]110[.]154

## Attribution (per Seqrite)

Seqrite states explicitly that they are not attributing the campaign to any known threat actor. Russian-language lures and regional targeting suggest a Russian-language operator but origin / sponsor is unspecified. Archimedes records Seqrite's non-attribution stance verbatim per Hard Rule 2. Analyst SAT-ACH may consider competing hypotheses (Russian internal/factional, false-flag, pro-Ukraine harvesting) but the finding does NOT promote any of these as Archimedes attribution.

## A&D-target relevance

- **Direct relevance: medium.** Eurasian unmanned aviation sector is a direct A&D-watchlist match. Targets are aviation professionals in UAV / aeronautical information services.
- **Boeing-aliased watchlist surface.** Boeing 737 navigation database lures = subject-matter aliasing of a Tier-1 prime watchlist entity. Lure content, not confirmed Boeing intrusion.
- **NOTAM datasets in lures** suggest the operator understands aviation-industry document conventions; targets are likely real aviation professionals.
- **Moscow forum timing** implies audience-specific operation, not opportunistic.
