## Technical detail

**CVE-2024-12802**
- CVSS v3.1: **9.1 CRITICAL** (NVD / CISA-ADP) vs. **6.5 MEDIUM**
  (SonicWall self-score) — 2.6-point divergence between two
  A-grade authorities
- CWE-305 Authentication Bypass by Primary Weakness
- Class: MFA bypass on SonicWall SSL-VPN when integrated with
  Microsoft Active Directory; arises from independent handling of
  UPN (user@domain.com) and SAM (DOMAIN\user) account-name formats
  in MFA enforcement

**Vulnerability mechanism (class-level, no PoC per Hard Rule 3):**
SonicWall SSL-VPN handles UPN and SAM account-name formats
SEPARATELY when integrated with AD. MFA enforcement can be
configured independently per login-name format. An attacker
holding valid credentials can submit one format that is NOT
MFA-protected on the appliance, bypassing the MFA control
defenders BELIEVE is enabled for the affected user.

**Incomplete-patching mitigation gap (freshly disclosed per
SonicWall PSIRT via BleepingComputer):**
- **Gen6 SSL-VPN**: firmware update alone DOES NOT fully mitigate.
  Customers must ALSO perform a manual LDAP server reconfiguration
  to enforce single-format account-name policy (UPN-only OR
  SAM-only at the AD + SSL-VPN config layer).
- **Gen7 and Gen8 SSL-VPN**: firmware update is sufficient.

ReliaQuest's incident-response evidence (per BleepingComputer
relay): in investigated environments, devices "appeared to be
patched because they were running the updated firmware, yet they
remained vulnerable because the required remediation steps had
not been completed." This is the operationally-consequential
finding — defenders who completed Step 1 (firmware) but skipped
Step 2 (LDAP reconfiguration) believed they were patched and
were not.
