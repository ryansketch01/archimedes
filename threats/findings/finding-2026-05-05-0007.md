# Microsoft MSTIC: multi-stage "code of conduct" AitM phishing campaign — 35K users, 13K orgs, 92% US (April 2026 burst)

## Summary

Microsoft Threat Intelligence and Defender Security Research published a joint 2026-05-04 writeup on a three-day phishing burst (2026-04-14 through 2026-04-16) that delivered adversary-in-the-middle session-token theft to 35,000+ recipients across 13,000+ organizations in 26 countries, with 92% of targets US-based. The chain runs compliance-themed lures ("Internal Regulatory COC," "Disciplinary Action") through Cloudflare-CAPTCHA-gated staging into a "Sign in with Microsoft" AitM proxy that intercepts the authenticated session and exfiltrates the token, bypassing non-phishing-resistant MFA. Microsoft does not attribute the activity to any tracked actor or Storm-NNNN cluster. Sectors named are healthcare and life sciences (19%), financial services (18%), professional services (11%), technology and software (11%) — defense, aerospace, and government are NOT named. SecurityWeek's same-day relay adds no independent observation; single effective primary source applies the single-source veto, capping WEP at "likely."

## Technical detail

- **Attribution (per source):** Unattributed. Microsoft does not assign a Storm-NNNN designation, does not name a nation-state, and uses qualified language ("likely originating from a cloud-hosted Windows virtual machine," "likely attacker-controlled" staging domains).
- **Campaign window:** 2026-04-14 through 2026-04-16 — three-day burst.
- **Scale:** 35,000+ recipients, 13,000+ organizations, 26 countries, 92% of targets US-based.
- **Sectors named (per Microsoft):** healthcare and life sciences (19%), financial services (18%), professional services (11%), technology and software (11%). **Defense, aerospace, and government are NOT named.**
- **Lure inventory:** display names "Internal Regulatory COC," "Workforce Communications," "Team Conduct Report"; subject lines reference conduct-policy violations. PDF attachments titled e.g. "Awareness Case Log File — [date].pdf," "Disciplinary Action — Employee Device Handling Case.pdf."
- **Chain:** PDF "Review Case Materials" link → Cloudflare CAPTCHA-gated landing → "encryption requirement" staging page → email-collection form → second image-selection CAPTCHA → platform-specific (mobile/desktop) redirect → "Sign in with Microsoft" AitM proxy → session token exfiltrated.
- **Bypass mechanism:** AitM reverse-proxy intercepts authenticated session and exfiltrates the token. Non-phishing-resistant MFA does not protect — credential entry occurs against an attacker-controlled proxy that completes the legitimate auth and captures the resulting session cookie.
- **MITRE mapping (paraphrased):** T1566.002 (spearphishing link), T1557 (adversary-in-the-middle), T1539 (steal web session cookie), T1078 (valid accounts).

## A&D relevance — mechanism, not observation

**Microsoft does not name defense or aerospace recipients.** The 92% US concentration is real but distributed across the four sectors above; A&D primes are not in that list.

The mechanism is platform-generic: any Entra ID tenant whose users authenticate with non-phishing-resistant MFA against a "Sign in with Microsoft" prompt is exposed to AitM token theft regardless of whether this specific campaign hit it. The compliance / "code of conduct" pretext shape routes naturally to legal, HR, and ethics-office staff. ITAR-regulated organizations carry above-baseline compliance traffic, which raises the conversion-rate ceiling for that pretext at primes. **This is forward-looking mechanism reasoning, not observed prime-direct activity** — briefer must keep the distinction explicit.
