# Morning Brief — 2026-07-31

**A fresh CISA advisory names the exact PLC families hit in a coordinated OT campaign against 30+ Minnesota water utilities — Rockwell CompactLogix/Micro850, Schneider Modicon M340, and Siemens S7-1200, all common on A&D manufacturing floors.**

**Why it matters:** Those controllers run defense-manufacturing and facility OT; CISA's three immediate hardening actions apply directly to any A&D operator with internet-reachable PLCs. No actor is attributed.

---

## 🚨 Active Threats

**UPDATE: CISA issues water-sector OT advisory after Minnesota PLC attacks**
- What: CISA published mitigation guidance (referencing advisory AA26-097A for IOCs) after coordinated attacks disrupted automated controls at 30+ Minnesota community water systems on July 26–27; attackers changed PLC passwords to lock out operators and swapped IP addresses to disconnect controllers, often entering via undocumented cellular modems.
- Why it matters for A&D: The named PLC families (Rockwell CompactLogix/Micro850, Schneider Modicon M340, Siemens S7-1200) run defense-manufacturing and facility OT — the same lock-out and disconnect tradecraft transfers to any exposed A&D control network.
- Action: Disconnect PLCs from the internet and route remote access through a VPN/gateway; change default credentials and enable password protection; allowlist IPs to known devices; keep clean PLC backups.
- Attribution: none. SecurityWeek names Iranian personas (CyberAv3ngers, Handala) only as profile-fit speculation; Archimedes originates and inherits none (Hard Rule 2). CISA's cross-reference to AA26-097A is a procedural IOC pointer, not an attribution of this campaign.
- Source: [SecurityWeek (Mike Lennon, July 30)](https://www.securityweek.com/cisa-urges-water-sector-to-protect-ot-after-coordinated-attacks-on-plcs/) · Digraph: B2

> 🔗 **Update on:** [2026-07-29 morning brief](2026-07-29-morning.md) — the Minnesota OT incident now carries an authoritative federal advisory and named PLC targets. Grade unchanged (B2, single-source veto still binds — CISA reached us through the same publisher).

## 🔓 Vulnerabilities

**UPDATE: JetBrains releases the fix for TeamCity RCE — CVE-2026-63077 (CVSS 9.8)**
- What: JetBrains shipped fixed versions 2025.11.7 and 2026.1.3, plus a security-patch plugin for 2017.1+ deployments; CVSS 9.8 is reaffirmed and the vendor reports no in-the-wild exploitation.
- Why it matters for A&D: An unauthenticated RCE in a CI/CD build server yields stored-credential theft and pipeline-integrity loss — the core ITAR-SDLC supply-chain risk (signing keys, artifact repos, deployment credentials).
- Action: Inventory internet-exposed TeamCity On-Premises; patch to 2025.11.7 / 2026.1.3 or apply the 2017.1+ plugin; VPN-gate access and run least-privilege. Patched with no ITW — not an emergency.
- Watch: Rapid7 notes prior TeamCity auth-bypass CVEs were weaponized quickly after disclosure; near-term exploitation of unpatched, exposed servers is **likely** if that base rate holds.
- Source: [Rapid7 Emergent Threat Response](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/etr-cve-2026-63077-critical-unauthenticated-remote-code-execution-in-jetbrains-teamcity) · [CVE-2026-63077](../vulnerabilities/_index.yaml) (dossier pending) · Digraph: A2
