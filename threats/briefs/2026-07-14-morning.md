# Morning Brief — 2026-07-14

**The Pentagon is suspending CMMC Phase 2 and standing up a task force to rewrite the rules** — a shift to the compliance regime that governs every DIB contractor, reported by SecurityWeek and not yet confirmed against a DoD primary.

**Why it matters:** CMMC is the assurance baseline your entire Tier-1/2 supplier network certifies against. Whether this is a full program halt or a rulemaking pause is unresolved — treat it as a planning signal to watch, not a green light to relax controls. A quiet day otherwise: three monitoring items, no action item.

## 🔓 Vulnerabilities

**[SAP July 2026 Patch Day fixes three critical flaws, led by a 9.9 in NetWeaver ABAP](https://www.securityweek.com/sap-patches-critical-vulnerabilities-in-netweaver-approuter-commerce-cloud/)**

- What: SAP patched **CVE-2026-44747** (CVSS 9.9, NetWeaver Application Server ABAP, memory corruption → unauthorized data access/modification and system unavailability), **CVE-2026-27690** (9.1, Approuter non-Cloud-Foundry, HTTP request smuggling → unauthenticated access), and **CVE-2026-44761** (9.1, Commerce Cloud, hardcoded OAuth2 credentials → unauthorized API access and data tampering). Patches are available; no source reports in-the-wild exploitation.
- Why it matters for A&D: SAP NetWeaver ABAP is the ERP backbone across large ITAR/defense-prime estates. No prime is named and no sector targeting is reported — the exposure is structural, not victim-anchored.
- Action: Patched is not handled. SAP ERP estates patch slowly (change control, regression testing), and the 2025 NetWeaver case (CVE-2025-31324) drew rapid exploitation of unpatched estates — prioritize the 9.9 ABAP flaw. A -30d Splunk sweep for the three CVEs returned zero hits; silence on a visibility-bounded host does not confirm a clean estate (Hard Rule 8).
- Source: [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/sap-patches-critical-vulnerabilities-in-netweaver-approuter-commerce-cloud/) (Onapsis technical analysis) · Digraph: B2 · WEP: likely
- Related: finding-2026-07-14-0002 · CVE-2026-44747 / CVE-2026-27690 / CVE-2026-44761 (net-new — vuln-tracker handoff pending, not yet in _index.yaml). Escalation tripwire: a CISA KEV add or A/B-grade ITW report on the 9.9 ABAP flaw re-opens FLASH eligibility.

## 🕵️ Actor Activity

**UPDATE: [Microsoft names Storm-3138 for the June Klue compromise inside a broader Salesforce OAuth-abuse campaign](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/13/defending-saas-based-applications-against-shinyhunters-oauth-abuse/)**

🔗 **Update on:** 2026-06-19 morning brief — the Klue/Salesforce supply-chain compromise (Icarus #025) now carries a second vendor's label and defensive guidance from an A-grade source.

- What's new: MSTIC published defensive guidance on a mid-2025→mid-2026 campaign abusing trusted OAuth relationships against Salesforce, and names **Storm-3138** for the June-2026 compromise of market-intelligence platform Klue — the incident Archimedes tracks as [Icarus (#025)](../threat-actors/Icarus/profile.md) via Huntress.
- Framing (load-bearing): Storm-3138 (Microsoft) and Icarus (Huntress) are two vendor labels on one incident — not a confirmed same actor. MSTIC clusters the wider campaign under tradecraft "commonly associated with ShinyHunters" — hedged tradecraft overlap, not an identity claim. Archimedes originates no merge (Hard Rule 2).
- Campaign shape: three OAuth-trust-abuse paths — vishing-driven consent abuse (a fake Salesforce Data Loader app), supply-chain compromise of trusted integrations (Salesloft Drift, August 2025; Gainsight, November 2025), and guest-access misconfiguration. MSTIC states this is not a Salesforce product flaw.
- Why it matters for A&D: no A&D prime is named (victims span retail, education, manufacturing) — exposure is structural. Any large ITAR enterprise running Salesforce plus OAuth-connected third-party apps shares the surface; audit connected-app inventory and integration OAuth scopes.
- Source: [Microsoft / MSTIC](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/13/defending-saas-based-applications-against-shinyhunters-oauth-abuse/) · Digraph: A2 · WEP: likely (single-source veto on the Storm-3138 label and campaign clustering)
- Related: [Icarus (#025)](../threat-actors/Icarus/profile.md) · finding-2026-07-14-0001 (same-incident label development on finding-2026-06-19-0003) · no atomic IOCs.
